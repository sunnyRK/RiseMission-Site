# risemission.app — independent audit

Audit date: 2026-11-15. Read-only; nothing was changed, committed or deployed.

**How this was checked**

- Crawled all 43 sitemap URLs (raw HTML, headers, JSON-LD, links) plus redirects, 404s, Markdown twins, llms.txt, RSS.
- Read the website source (`/Users/sunnyradadiya/IOS-App/RiseMission-Website`, HEAD `1d0c626`).
- Verified product claims against the app at git ref `v1.5` (`e20346e`).
- Rendered the App Store listing (App Privacy label, IAP list, ratings) and called the iTunes lookup API.
- Fetched alar.my, erly.co, risly.app (and Risly's en-US sitemap) and ran three web searches.
- Rendered the homepage and one post at 390x844 in a headless browser.

**What could not be measured**

- **PageSpeed Insights / Lighthouse:** the keyless API returned "Quota exceeded" on all three calls. No Lighthouse scores and no CrUX field data exist in this report. Lab numbers below are from an unthrottled headless browser on a fast connection and are not a substitute.
- **Search Console, rankings, traffic, backlinks, AI-citation data:** not available. Nothing in this report estimates them.
- **Index status:** the Next.js site went live today (merge commit `b6f6d39`, 2026-11-15 15:37 +0530), so nothing new can be indexed yet.
- **Sleep Foundation, Omni and vclock** were not fetched; they are mentioned only as incumbents for the calculator/online-alarm queries.
- **Instagram** (`@risemissionapp`) sits behind a login wall; its activity and follower count are unverified.

Labels used: **[V]** = verified with evidence in this audit. **[S]** = suggestion or judgement.

## B. Critical problems (verified)

### B1. The core promise is not true as written [V]

The site says the alarm "keeps ringing until you prove you're awake" and is "the alarm you can't snooze away".

- `src/app/page.tsx:16`, `:21`, `:73-80` (meta description, H1, lead)
- `src/app/llms.txt/route.ts:13` ("The alarm keeps ringing until you complete a wake-up challenge")
- `src/lib/media.ts:38`, `src/app/gallery/page.tsx:11`

The app's own documentation at `v1.5` says otherwise. `docs/TECHNICAL_LIMITATIONS.md`, hard limit 1: "The system Stop button always exists… A user can stop the alarm without opening the challenge. Rise Mission records such wake-ups honestly… rather than claiming the alarm is 'impossible to disable'." The code agrees: `RiseMission/App/AppRouter.swift:159-168` (`handleSystemStopped`) records `.stoppedWithoutChallenge`.

The only place the website admits this is one sentence on `/support` (`src/app/support/page.tsx:37-38`). No homepage section, FAQ, challenge guide or comparison post mentions it. Meanwhile `src/lib/competitors.ts:397` calls a competitor's "no Stop button" wording an unverified developer claim.

Consequences: AI answers will repeat "cannot be dismissed without the challenge"; buyers who discover the Stop slider after paying have grounds for refunds and 1-star reviews; it is also App Review exposure (accurate metadata).

### B2. The iOS 26 requirement is absent from visible copy on 33 of 43 pages [V]

`MIN_IOS` (`src/lib/site.ts:29`) is used only in JSON-LD (`src/lib/seo.ts:127`) and llms.txt. The string "iOS 26" appears zero times in the visible `<main>` of:

- the homepage, `/support`, `/about`, `/gallery`, `/tools` and all six tools
- all four challenge guides (push-up, QR, math, step)
- all seven topic hubs and several other posts

It appears only in the comparison posts and the iPhone-troubleshooting posts. The homepage also never says "not on Android" or "iPhone only" in plain words. A visitor on iOS 18 learns this at the App Store. An answer engine asked "which iPhones can use Rise Mission" has no page to quote.

### B3. Privacy policy is stale and omits data the App Privacy label declares [V]

- `/privacy` says "Updated August 21, 2026" (`src/app/privacy/page.tsx:14`). v1.5 shipped 2026-09-05 with Morning Briefing.
- v1.5 requests **Location** and **Calendar** (`Info.plist`: `NSLocationWhenInUseUsageDescription`, `NSCalendarsFullAccessUsageDescription`; `Services/MorningBriefing.swift:180-181` calls WeatherKit with the location). Neither permission is in the policy's Permissions section (`privacy/page.tsx:102-110`), and weather/location/calendar are not mentioned anywhere on the page.
- The App Store label (rendered today) declares, as "Data Not Linked to You": Purchase History, **Coarse Location**, Device ID, Product Interaction, for Analytics, App Functionality and **Developer's Advertising or Marketing**.
- The site's summary line calls analytics "anonymous" (`src/lib/site.ts:51`). Apple's wording is "not linked to your identity", and Device ID is declared for advertising. "No personal content leaves your device" (`privacy/page.tsx:96`) sits awkwardly next to coarse location going to Apple's weather service.
- The sitemap gives `/privacy` a lastmod of 2026-11-15 (`src/app/sitemap.ts:19`) while the page itself says August 21.
- What is consistent: no "Data Used to Track You" section on the label, matching the site's "never tracks you across other apps".
- App-side note: the location usage string in `Info.plist` says "never uploaded", which conflicts with the label declaring Coarse Location as collected.

### B4. "Voice stays on your iPhone" is stated unconditionally; the code is conditional [V]

`RiseMission/Services/SpeechService.swift:39` sets `requiresOnDeviceRecognition = recognizer.supportsOnDeviceRecognition`. Where on-device recognition is unsupported, audio goes to Apple's servers. `/privacy` hedges correctly ("wherever your iPhone supports it"). These do not:

- `src/lib/site.ts:49` (badge "Camera & voice stay on your iPhone")
- `src/lib/site.ts:51` (used in the home FAQ and llms.txt)
- `src/lib/site.ts:73` ("on-device speech recognition checks it")
- `/support` ("your iPhone recognizes it on-device")

### B5. Competitor data understates competitors, and the comparison posts lean on it [V]

- **Alarmy:** `src/lib/competitors.ts:104` lists 5 missions (Math, Memory, Shake, Squats, Photo). Alarmy's own site lists 8: math, memory, shake, typing, squat, QR/barcode, walking, photo. The `/blog/rise-mission-vs-alarmy` FAQ says Rise Mission "includes QR, walk, voice, typing and memory challenges" as a difference, and "You like variety" is listed as a Rise Mission win. Alarmy has four of those five, and Memory is even in the site's own Alarmy row.
- **Erly:** `competitors.ts:133` has `missions: []`. erly.co names push-ups (camera-verified), math, Bible verses, sky photos, item searches, make your bed, touch grass and affirmations. The vs-Erly post does cite erly.co in its table, but its H1 still frames the choice as "Streaks or Challenge Variety?".
- **The roundup's method** ("we searched the US App Store…") omits the closest competitors. Apple's "You Might Also Like" on Rise Mission's own listing shows Risly, RiseReps, Pushy, Rise & Grind, IronWake, Nooze, Wakeuply. "Risly" appears 0 times in `src/`.
- **Camera-counted push-ups are not a differentiator.** Risly, Erly, Pushy, WakeFit, Ruffup and RiseReps all advertise it. Verified differentiators are narrower: 20.1 MB (Risly is 216.4 MB, Alarmy 250.8 MB), a lifetime purchase option, and the breadth of camera/voice/walk challenges in one small app.

The "listing only" rule is defensible as a method, but the result reads as selective. One fact-check by a reader or an AI engine undermines the "honest comparison" positioning.

### B6. No measurement on the live site [V]

The live homepage HTML contains no `googletagmanager` and no PostHog. All 13 App Store links are the bare URL: `NEXT_PUBLIC_APPSTORE_PT` is unset, so `appStoreHref()` (`src/components/site/app-store-button.tsx:4-9`) adds no `pt`/`ct`. The site launched without any way to know which page produces installs.

### B7. Launch pattern contradicts the site's own playbook [V]

`CLAUDE.md` and `docs/SEO_PLAYBOOK.md` §5 say "max 3–5 new URLs per week… never mass-generate". The live site went from 4 pages to 43 URLs in one day.

- All 21 posts carry `date: "2026-11-15"`; most of the long-form content arrived in a single commit (`b54cbe5`).
- Every post has exactly one image, the generated cover. No screenshots, photos or original test data appear in any article.
- `/about` says "I write the guides on this site" and the playbook requires "first-person operator voice". The posts read as careful desk research.
- Rise Mission vs Alarmy is 1,083 words against the repo's own 1,500-word floor.

I cannot verify how the posts were authored, and the sourcing discipline is good. The risk is how this looks to Google: a low-authority domain publishing roughly 45k words in a day, on the same topics as incumbents. Expect slow indexing ("Discovered – currently not indexed").

### B8. Money pages have the fewest internal links; "Latest" is alphabetical [V]

Inlinks counted from the crawl, out of 42 other pages:

| Page | Pages linking to it |
|---|---|
| `/blog/iphone-alarm-volume-low` | 4 |
| `/blog/rise-mission-vs-alarmy` | 5 |

Because every post has the same date, `sortedPosts()` in `src/lib/blog.ts` falls back to slug order. The homepage "guides" block (`src/app/page.tsx:54`) and the nav "Latest" column (`src/lib/nav.ts:53`, `:91`) therefore permanently show ADHD, get-out-of-bed and Alarmy alternatives. The nav also labels items "Most used" (`nav.ts:58`, `:74`) with no data behind it.

## C. Missing opportunities

1. **No pricing answer anywhere.** The US listing publicly shows the IAP list (today: Weekly $0.99, Yearly $9.99, Monthly $9.99, Lifetime $49.99, and a legacy "Rise Mission Pro" $9.99). The StoreKit test file in the repo has different numbers, so confirm in App Store Connect. Any assistant can read the listing. The site declines to answer "how much does Rise Mission cost", does not state the trial length (the StoreKit file shows 3 days on yearly), and does not say plainly that the app cannot be used without Pro.
2. **A definition page for the entity.** The brand is easy to confuse: a search for the app name returned RiseUp: Mission Alarm Clock, Rise – Motivational Alarm, Mission – Alarm Clock and Rise Alarm Clock, and not Rise Mission. The H1 is a slogan with no "iPhone alarm clock app" in it. Nothing gives an engine one unambiguous paragraph: what it is, platform, iOS version, developer, App Store ID, price model, what it is not.
3. **Six challenges have no guide.** Squats, Shake, Object Find, Voice, Typing and Memory link to homepage anchors (`src/lib/nav.ts:49`).
4. **Lifetime / no-subscription angle.** It is a verified difference from Risly (monthly/yearly only per its listing) and is barely used.
5. **Size angle.** The data page exists, but "small alarm app" and "alarm app that doesn't take up storage" have no landing copy on the homepage.
6. **Morning routine.** The owner's target query has no page, although v1.5's Morning Briefing is the natural hook.
7. **No third-party presence.** No AlternativeTo, Product Hunt, press or review-site mentions surfaced in searches. Answer engines weight corroboration; on-site content alone rarely earns a "best alarm app" citation.
8. **Localisation.** Risly publishes 18 locale sitemaps. Rise Mission is English-only in both app and site.
9. **Reviews.** v1.5 has a review prompt (`Services/ReviewPrompter.swift`), yet the US count is 0. Once ratings exist they unlock `aggregateRating`, homepage proof and a row in the comparison tables.

## G. Verdict

The engineering is better than most indie app sites: fast, clean, structurally correct, and unusually disciplined about sources and disclosure.

The weakness is what the site says and who backs it up. The headline promise is stronger than the product can deliver on iOS. A hard device requirement is hidden. The privacy policy lags the shipped app. The comparisons are honest in tone but incomplete in fact. Around all of that there is no rating, no review, no third-party mention and no analytics.

Risly's site already has the same tools and many of the same post slugs, press logos and 18 languages. Alarmy has the authority. As launched, Rise Mission is a 43-page site with no evidence of experience entering their SERPs. It is unlikely to rank or be cited on its content alone in the short term. That is a judgement, not a measurement.

**The three changes that matter most**

1. **Make every claim exactly true and complete.** The Stop slider, iOS 26, pricing and paywall, privacy, competitor missions. This is the precondition for being quoted accurately and for the "honest alarm app" positioning to work.
2. **Get proof from outside the site.** Ratings, a handful of real reviews and listings, plus first-hand evidence inside the posts. Without corroboration the AEO mechanics have nothing to amplify.
3. **Measure, then concentrate.** Turn on analytics and campaign links today. Push internal links and effort toward the five or six commercial pages (homepage, pricing, roundup, vs Alarmy, vs Apple Clock, push-up guide) and stop adding informational posts.
